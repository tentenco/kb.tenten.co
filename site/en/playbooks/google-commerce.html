Google Ads and product feeds

Connect conversions, product data and landing pages before spending.

Understand the conceptMain pointsTake actionCheck the result

Start here

What is it?

Google Ads runs campaigns; Merchant Center supplies product data. Accurate products and purchase reports provide the foundation.

When to use it

Use this for Shopping setup, disapproved products or duplicate conversions.

Why it matters

  • Missing conversions weaken bidding signals; incorrect feeds can cause disapproval or price mismatches.
  • Separate integration acceptance from campaign activation so spending has measurable outcomes.

What to know

Remember these 5 points

  1. Assign each platform a job

    Ads handles campaigns and conversions; Merchant Center handles products and eligibility; GA4 analyzes behavior; GSC covers search indexing.

  2. Avoid duplicate bidding signals

    Avoid using both native Ads and imported GA4 purchases as primary bidding conversions for the same purchase. Check how observation actions enter campaign goals.

  3. Match the feed to the site

    Keep stable IDs, applicable brand/GTIN identifiers, price, currency, stock, images, live URLs and shipping/return policies consistent.

  4. Use first-party data appropriately

    Enhanced conversions use approved first-party data for matching. Hashing does not remove consent or data responsibilities.

  5. Test official integrations

    Consider Shopify’s official Google & YouTube integration first. Test headless checkout, domains and product links end to end.

Glossary 3 terms
Feed
A structured product catalog supplied to a platform.
GTIN
A global trade item number, supplied when applicable.
Primary conversion
An outcome used for bidding, such as a purchase.

In practice

An example

The feed says “in stock” while the landing page says “sold out.”

What to take from this

Keep catalog, page and checkout information consistent.

Put it to work

Start here

Identify the existing Ads, Merchant Center and GA4 accounts and connections.

Before you start 2 items
  • Existing Ads, Merchant Center and GA4 accounts; target markets
  • Product identifiers, shipping/return policies, conversion definitions and authorization
  1. Inspect links and conversion actions. Define one primary purchase source with value, currency and transaction_id.
  2. Sync products through supported integrations/APIs and inspect diagnostics. Do not invent GTINs or submit development URLs.
  3. Test order reconciliation, reload deduplication, cross-domain attribution and consent. Verify current enhanced-conversion support.
  4. Deliver eligibility and disapproval findings with fixes. Activate budgets, assets and campaigns only within advertising authorization.

How to check the result

  • ✓Sample prices, stock and URLs match landing pages; disapprovals have a reason and action.
  • ✓One purchase creates the intended bidding signal. Integration completion does not establish campaign results.

Decisions to make

Agree on ad goals, conversion values and budget before authorized activation.

Common pitfalls

  • Counting one order in multiple primary conversion actions.
  • Pointing feeds to a Shopify theme when customers should use the custom storefront.
Sources and scope

Reviewed 2026-10-21 · Suggested guidance; adapt to the project

  • Google Merchant Center: product data specification ↗Product identity, price, currency, stock, links and consistency. Does not guarantee approval.
  • Google Ads: enhanced conversions ↗First-party matching, hashing and enhanced conversions. Data-use and consent obligations still apply.
  • Shopify: Google & YouTube channel ↗Official channel setup and product-sync documentation. Does not verify a store’s eligibility or conversions.
  • Google Analytics: ecommerce events ↗Ecommerce events, items, currency, transaction_id, purchases and refunds. Check current Meta/Ads documents when implementing those platforms.
